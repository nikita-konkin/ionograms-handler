Hue is the parameter, shape is the source. Markers are measured, lines are modelled — so a blue marker and the blue dashed line beside it are this circuit's MUF and IRI's, and the gap between them is what the residual panel plots. {% if frame.circuits|length > 1 %} With several circuits overlaid, hue is the circuit instead — two paths' MUFs in one colour is exactly the comparison this must not invite — so only MUF and its model are drawn at first. Each circuit is modelled at its own control point; the legend groups them. {% endif %}

Hollow markers are bounds, not measurements. A hollow MUF marker sat at the top of the sweep, so the real MUF is at or above it; a hollow LOF marker sat at the band floor, so the real LOF is at or below it. They are drawn rather than filtered out — dropping either silently would bend the curve towards the middle of the band. They are left out of the residual statistics below, which is a different decision and is stated there.

foF2 here is not measured. An oblique sounder never sees vertical incidence. It is the measured MUF put back through the secant law at hmF2 = {{ '%.0f'|format(frame.hmf2_km) }} km over one hop, which is what makes it comparable with a model or with a nearby vertical ionosonde. IRI's own foF2 is its model output, converted the same way in reverse to get the MUF beside it.

LOF, not LUF. ITU-R P.533-13 sec. 9 defines the lowest usable frequency with a required signal-to-noise ratio and a monthly median — a property of a service and of a month, neither of which one sounding has. What an oblique sounder scales is the lowest observed frequency, and that is what this is. It tracks D-region absorption, so it follows the sun rather than the F2 layer: a MUF that moves while the LOF under it does not is worth a second look.

Bias is a median of measured − IRI, over the pairs that are measurements. The lower bounds counted in the at ceiling column are excluded from it: a pick pinned to the top of the sweep says the ionosphere supported at least that, and scoring it as a residual would report the recorder's band ceiling as a modelling error. On a ceiling-limited circuit that is most of the daytime, which is why the count of excluded points is printed beside the one used.

IRI is a climatology driven by a smoothed solar index, and for a recent month that index does not exist yet — see muf/reference/indices.py. Treat a few MHz of disagreement as normal and the shape of the residual as the interesting part: a flat offset is a scale question, a diurnal one is not.
